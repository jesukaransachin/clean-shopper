## 1. Problem Statement
People trying to make informed purchasing decisions for their home and family — replacing toxic cleaning products, finding personal care items without harmful chemicals, or stocking a cleaner pantry — find the research overwhelming and time-consuming. Ingredient lists require expertise to interpret, "clean" and "natural" label claims are often misleading or unverified, and there is no single place to track the preferences (trusted brands, avoided ingredients) built up through that research.

## 2. ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)
Someone actively trying to replace toxic cleaning products, find personal care items free of harmful chemicals, or stock a pantry with cleaner food options. They currently research alternatives by piecing together multiple fragmented sources rather than relying on a single trusted tool, and over time they accumulate preferences — trusted brands, ingredients to avoid — that today have no persistent home.

## 3. Pain Points
- Product labels are confusing and ingredient lists require expertise to interpret.
- "Clean" and "natural" claims on packaging are often misleading or unverified.
- Researching alternatives across multiple sources is fragmented and slow.
- Preferences built up over time — trusted brands, avoided ingredients — have nowhere to live.

## 4. Proposed Solution
Clean Shopper is a conversational interface backed by Claude that lets a user:
- Describe what they're looking for and receive researched product options, with ingredients evaluated against clean standards and recommendations presented with reasoning (drawing on real-time web search and databases such as EWG's Skin Deep).
- Save preferences — ingredients to avoid, trusted brands, and certifications that matter to them (e.g., EWG Verified, USDA Organic, B Corp) — and have those preferences applied automatically to every subsequent recommendation.
- Add recommended products to a shopping cart that persists across sessions.
- Ask Clean Shopper to compare products side by side and receive a clear recommendation based on their saved preferences.

Explicitly out of scope for V1: checkout or payment processing, direct retailer integrations, barcode scanning, user accounts or authentication, and a mobile app.

## 5. Success Metrics
Success is measured through engagement and retention rather than revenue or conversion, consistent with this being a course demo project. Key indicators: weekly active users, repeat/return sessions, number of preferences saved per user, and frequency of returning to add or compare products across sessions.

## 6. Design Constraints
Platform: Conversational interface backed by Claude. Mobile app explicitly out of scope for V1; no other platform detail (e.g., web vs. desktop) specified.
Geography: US only. Certifications referenced (USDA Organic, EWG Verified) are US-centric, so ingredient data, certification matching, and recommendations are scoped to the US market.
Accessibility: Standard web accessibility baseline — WCAG 2.1 AA.
Technical: Backed by Claude; uses real-time web search and draws on external ingredient-safety databases such as EWG's Skin Deep. Built using Claude Code as the course demo project for "Claude Code for Designers," developed by students over four weeks with the instructor demonstrating the workflow live each session.
Other: User accounts/authentication, checkout/payment processing, direct retailer integrations, and barcode scanning are all explicitly excluded from V1 scope. Cart must persist across sessions despite no user accounts being in scope. This is a pure course exercise — a teaching vehicle for the four-week Claude Code course, not intended to map to a real product, business, or monetization strategy.
